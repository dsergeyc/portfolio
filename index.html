Case Study 03
Unified Paywall
Role
Lead Designer

Designed in partnership with the Growth design team.

Problem
Every product team was building its own paywall.
  • Photoshop, Acrobat, Firefly, and Illustrator each shipped their own upgrade experiences on web
  • The same design, legal, and pricing requirements were re-solved from scratch by every team
  • Nothing carried across — an experiment that worked for one product never reached the others
Insight
The paywall is the step before checkout — and every team already used our checkout.

Adoption wasn't a selling problem. If the platform shipped a paywall the same way it shipped checkout — same integration path, same expectations — product teams would take it the same way.

Solution
One paywall platform, adopted across Adobe.
What the platform provides
  • A configurable paywall that hands off directly into Adobe's shared checkout
  • Spectrum components, so every paywall inherits Adobe's design standards
  • Legal, pricing, and compliance requirements built in rather than re-implemented
  • Experimentation hooks each product's growth team can run against
My role
  • Led design end to end, from platform concept through launch
  • Designed the self-service configuration experience
  • Defined the guardrails that keep flexibility from becoming fragmentation
  • Established the component contribution model with partner teams
In Production
Firefly's paywall — configured, not custom-built.
In Production
The same paywall on desktop.
Guardrails
What teams control

Copy, imagery, offers, and layout options — changed in their own CMS, on their own schedule.

What the platform holds

The component library, design standards, legal and pricing requirements, and the handoff into checkout.

Why the line matters

Too rigid and no one adopts it. Too loose and it stops being a platform. The guardrails are what let every team move independently without drifting apart.

Self-Service CMS
Teams run their own paywalls.

An internal management system where each product team picks a template, configures the paywall, and edits the copy — without a platform ticket or a release train.

Choose a template

Teams start from the platform's approved layouts — the fastest path to a paywall that already meets Adobe's standards.

Configure the paywall

Plans, offers, and which options surface — set per product, and changed whenever the growth team needs a new test.

Edit the copy

Headlines, benefit lines, and CTAs are owned by the product team, so messaging moves at their pace rather than ours.

AI Growth Experimentation
Internal Tool
Role
Design Manager
Problem
Running an A/B test on Adobe.com takes weeks.
  • Analytics, design reviews, and campaign setup each require a separate team and handoff
  • Bottlenecks compound — slow cycle times mean fewer experiments per quarter
  • PMs can't ship tests fast enough to keep up with the optimization roadmap
Solution
One conversation. Under five minutes.
What COMEX does
  • Identifies funnel friction from live analytics
  • Generates and prioritizes hypotheses by expected impact
  • Creates design variants using Spectrum components
  • Configures targeting, traffic split, guardrails, and success metrics
My role
  • Defined the product concept and end-to-end workflow
  • Designed the AI assistant UX and approval interface
  • Built the Control vs Challenger comparison view
  • Partnered with PM and Engineering on the agentic pipeline
Interface

Interface

How It Works
From friction to approved test in minutes.
Find friction

COMEX reads Analytics and surfaces the highest-signal drop-off points across the funnel — automatically ranked by conversion opportunity.

Generate the treatment

The assistant produces multiple design challengers using Spectrum components and shows them side-by-side with the control — no Figma required for approval.

Configure and launch

Campaign setup — targeting, traffic allocation, guardrails, and metrics — is pre-populated and ready to approve. PM reviews the packet and ships.
